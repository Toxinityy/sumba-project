Hope for Sumba — three visual directions

This page presents three complete, self-contained visual languages for the same site content — the same hero, school card, stat band, pull quote and call-to-action, rendered three times. Nothing here is final; nothing here is code for the live site. It exists so Reynold and the team can react to a feeling before a single line of production CSS is written.

All three directions share the fixed constraints already decided: Fraunces for display type, Plus Jakarta Sans for body and UI, the 8px spacing system, and the bilingual sizing rule (Indonesian text is never squeezed to fit English). What differs between them is color, contrast of mood, and the small structural decisions — corner radius, image treatment, rhythm — that make a site feel like one thing instead of a template.

How to read this
  1. Read each direction's one-paragraph rationale before looking at the colors — the palette is chosen to serve the feeling, not the other way round.
  2. Every color pairing used for real text carries its measured contrast ratio and a PASS/FAIL against the WCAG AA threshold that applies to it (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large/bold text 18px and up). None of the three directions ships a failing pair.
  3. Photography is represented as labeled tonal placeholders, not real images — real photography is being shot separately in Sumba. Judge the palette on its own terms.
1. Open Field 2. Limestone & Tide 3. Dusk Savanna

Direction 1 of 3

Open Field

The target feeling is unhurried competence — the register of a well-run field office, not a campaign. A warm, slightly green-cast paper stands in for savanna daylight without tipping into travel-brochure tropical color; a single ochre gold, drawn from dry grass rather than fruit or brass, carries every call to action. Deep forest ink replaces true black, so nothing on the page shouts. For a due-diligence reader, this is the direction that says "we have been doing this work for years and we are not trying to convince you of anything" — restraint reads as a track record, not as a design choice.

Direction 2 of 3

Limestone & Tide

Where Open Field is warm and grounded, this direction is cool and precise — the limestone cliffs and turquoise water of Sumba's coastline rather than its grasslands. Sharper corners, tighter grid lines and a single deep teal give the site a clinical clarity that reads as operational rigor: numbers add up, reports arrive on time, the organization runs the way a diligence panel wants a partner to run. It is the more contemporary, almost civic-tech register of the three — appropriate if the founders want the site to feel closer to a modern foundation's annual report than a photo essay.

Direction 3 of 3

Dusk Savanna

The most distinctive of the three: a dark, warm register built from the color of Sumba at last light, when the grassland and the sky turn the same deep amber-brown and the thatch roofline of an uma mbatangu sits as a silhouette against it. This is the direction with the most nerve — it trades the safety of a white background for a sense of place no generic NGO template would risk. Used correctly (as a home-page mood, not as every inner page) it signals a founder who has actually been to Sumba and is not afraid to let the site look like it, which is its own form of credibility with a skeptical funder who has seen a hundred identical white nonprofit sites.
